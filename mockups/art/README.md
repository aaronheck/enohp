# Yelnats hero art: 5 concepts

These are home-screen mocks at 390×844 (`concept-N.png`) plus a 375×560 check (`concept-N-small.png`). `sheet.png` puts them side by side. Each art piece is one inline SVG: `viewBox="0 0 340 260"`, `preserveAspectRatio="xMidYMid meet"`, clipped to the viewBox. It sits in the real `.home-art` flex box (min 120, max 340), so it never reaches the buttons. Everything uses flat Party Line colours and Bricolage Grotesque, with no gradients. The HTML/SVG was generated by a throwaway script at `/tmp/yel/gen/build.py`, which isn't part of the repo.

## 1. Letter Swap (typographic, animated)
**Idea:** STANLEY flips into YELNATS, tile by tile.
The game's name is its own rule, so the hero spells it out: cream letter tiles read STANLEY. Two crossing arrows (tomato and sky) carry the S and the Y to opposite ends, around a rewind badge, and the result is YELNATS in butter tiles. The tiles get the same 4px "press" shadow as the buttons, so the art matches the controls and feels tappable. **Motion:** the bottom tiles flip in one at a time like a split-flap board, then the row settles with a small wobble. On the Sent screen the tiles could spell the player's own word and its reversal, or flip "PASS IT ON" → "NO TI SSAP".

## 2. Whisper Line (characters)
**Idea:** three bean-shaped party guests pass a word down the line, and it drifts.
This is the only concept that shows the social, party-game side: a chain of friends and family, each a little off. The bubbles drift from "pickle jar" to "tickle car" to a puzzled lilac bean saying "nick's cage?", so the joke and the rules come across in one glance. **Motion:** each bean bobs while its bubble pops in, in turn. The last bean squints, then the loop restarts. On the Sent screen, add a dashed empty bean with a "?" bubble at the end: "you're next / they're next".

## 3. Rewind Reels (object, animated)
**Idea:** two bare tape reels spinning backwards, joined by the vibrating string.
This takes the recording UI's tomato dot, butter dot and cream wiggle line and turns them into reels. The hero and the record screen then share one visual language. It's very simple and stays readable as a thumbnail. **Motion:** the reels turn counter-clockwise and the tape wiggles like the live voice line. On the Sent screen they spin forward and the tape winds over to the right-hand reel, a literal hand-off. Risk: at a glance it reads as a film reel or a generic "media" icon.

## 4. Round Trip (speech, mirror, ear)
**Idea:** a laughing mouth, a mirror line, an ear: what you say comes out the other side reversed.
It shows the mechanic itself. The butter waveform (the same smooth mirrored shape used for playback) hits the vibrating-string "mirror" and comes out flipped in sky, straight into an ear. **Motion:** the waveform flows from the mouth, changes colour as it crosses the mirror, and the ear gives a little twitch. On the Sent screen, swap the mouth and ear to show the turn passing on. Risk: loose body parts can look odd to some people, and it explains more than it entertains.

## 5. Drift Trail (the recap as a map)
**Idea:** the chain as a board-game path, each stop a guess further from the start.
It previews the payoff, the recap, as a winding game-board road. Numbered player tokens and cream word pills ("pickle jar" → "tickle car" → "nickel bar") end at a butter "nick's cage" and a "?" token. **Motion:** a token hops along the road stop by stop and each pill pops in. On the Sent screen, the road extends past your stop to an empty dashed "?" stop for the next player. Weakness: the word pills get very small on short phones.

## Recommendation
**Pursue 1 (Letter Swap), with 2 (Whisper Line) as the warmer alternative.**

- **Letter Swap** has the most ownable idea. The name *is* the mechanic, nobody else can use it, and it looks like part of the UI (press-shadowed tiles) rather than an advert. It's also the easiest to animate well, and the letters stay readable on the 560px screen. For the Sent screen, re-spelling the player's own word gives a nice personal moment.
- **Whisper Line** best matches the "playful, absurd, warm" tone and is the only one with people in it. It explains the game with a joke. On very short screens the words get tiny, so I'd let the bubbles fade out below about 160px of art height and keep only the beans.

I'd drop 3 (too generic a "media" symbol) and 4 (more diagram than delight). 5 could come back later as the art for the recap screen.
